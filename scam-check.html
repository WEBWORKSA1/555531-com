Was that call a scam?

Scammers change numbers every hour but their script barely changes. Answer what happened on the call — not who it claimed to be — and get a clear verdict with next steps.

The one rule that beats every scam

Hang up, then contact the person or company using a number you look up. A real bank, agency or relative will still be there in five minutes.

Why we score behaviour, not the number

The FTC’s reports show phone-initiated scams have the highest median loss of any contact method — thousands of dollars per victim — even though texts and social media reach more people. The reason is pressure: a live voice can push you past your own judgement. That pressure leaves fingerprints, and those are what the checker weighs.

The heaviest signals

  • Untraceable payment — gift cards, crypto, wire transfers, payment apps or a courier collecting cash. No legitimate agency or company asks for these over the phone.
  • Reading back a code — one-time passcodes exist so that only you can approve a login or payment. Anyone who asks for one is trying to become you.
  • Remote access — “tech support” or “bank security” asking you to install AnyDesk, TeamViewer or a similar app.
  • A loved one in distress — AI voice cloning has turbo-charged the old grandparent scam. Agree a family safe word.

If you already paid

Speed matters more than anything else. Call your bank or card issuer using the number on the back of your card, report the transaction as fraud, and ask whether it can be recalled. Then report the scam — reports are how agencies spot patterns and how call-blocking apps learn new numbers. Our reporting guide lists the right place in each country.
