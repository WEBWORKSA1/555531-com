The codes that look domestic but aren’t

Twenty-plus countries and territories share the +1 country code with the US and Canada. A call from 876 (Jamaica), 809 / 829 / 849 (Dominican Republic), 473 (Grenada), 649 (Turks and Caicos) or 284 (British Virgin Islands) looks like an ordinary long-distance call. It isn’t: it is international, and calling back can be billed at international or premium rates.

That is the engine of the one-ring or “wangiri” scam — the phone rings once, you see a missed call, and curiosity does the rest. Our guide One-ring scams and the area codes behind them explains how to protect yourself and what to do if you already called back.

Toll-free and 900

800, 833, 844, 855, 866, 877 and 888 are toll-free — free for you to call, paid by the business. They are also the most commonly spoofed numbers in “your account is locked” scams. 900 is the opposite: the caller pays, often by the minute.

How an area code is built

A North American number has three parts: NPA (area code), NXX (exchange) and the 4-digit line number. Neither the area code nor the exchange may begin with 0 or 1, and an exchange can’t be an N11 service code like 911. Numbers that break these rules are invalid — and often spoofed.
