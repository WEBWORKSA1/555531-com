The reserved ranges, and where they come from

United States and Canada: 555-0100 to 555-0199

Hollywood has used the 555 exchange since the early 1960s because the letters on the 5 key — J, K, L — matched almost no real exchange names. In the 1990s most of the 555 exchange was opened for real services, and today only 555-0100 through 555-0199 remain reserved for fiction in every North American area code. A number like 555-2368 may look fake, but it is not protected.

United Kingdom: Ofcom drama numbers

Ofcom keeps 1,000-number blocks for TV and radio drama in major cities — for example London 020 7946 0000–0999, Manchester 0161 496 0000–0999 and Glasgow 0141 496 0000–0999 — plus the area-less 01632 960000–960999, and 07700 900000–900999 for mobiles.

Ireland and Australia

Ireland’s regulator ComReg reserves 020 91X XXXX for drama use. Australia’s ACMA publishes a specific list of mobile and 1800 numbers for creative works.

Using fictional numbers in software

  • Seed staging databases and screenshots with these numbers so no real customer ever receives a test SMS.
  • Note that strict validators (including Google’s libphonenumber) may mark some fiction-only numbers as invalid — that is a feature for production, so mock validation in unit tests.
  • Store numbers in E.164 and keep test data clearly labelled.

Writers: avoid the “Jenny” problem

When a real number appears on screen or in a book, its owner gets the calls. Productions have had to re-dub scenes and pay for number changes after using live numbers. Using a reserved range costs nothing and removes the risk.
