What a SIM swap is

Your phone number lives with your carrier, not in your phone. In a SIM swap, a criminal convinces your carrier — by social engineering, a bribed insider or stolen account details — to move your number to a SIM card or eSIM they control. In a port-out, they transfer your number to a different carrier. Either way, your phone suddenly loses service, and calls and texts, including one-time login codes, go to the attacker.

The prize is usually your email, bank, investment or crypto accounts that use SMS codes for login or password resets. In 2023 the US FCC adopted rules requiring wireless carriers to use secure methods to authenticate customers before SIM changes and port-outs and to notify customers when they are requested.

Warning signs

  • Your phone shows “No service” or “SOS only” in an area where it normally works.
  • You get a text or email from your carrier about a SIM change or port request you didn’t make.
  • Password-reset emails or login alerts you didn’t trigger.
  • Friends say they received odd messages from your number.

Five protections to set today

  1. Carrier PIN or passcode. Set a unique account PIN — not your birthday or the last four of your ID number.
  2. Port-out / number lock. Most major carriers offer a free toggle that blocks transfers until you switch it off.
  3. Move off SMS codes. Use an authenticator app, a passkey or a security key for email, banking and crypto. Keep SMS only as a last-resort backup.
  4. Hide your number. Don’t list your main mobile publicly; use a secondary number for shopping and sign-ups.
  5. Bank alerts. Turn on instant alerts for logins, new payees and transfers.

If it happens

  1. Contact your carrier immediately from another phone or in store; ask them to reverse the change and lock the account.
  2. From a trusted device, change your email password first (it resets everything else), then banking and crypto.
  3. Call your bank’s fraud line to freeze transfers.
  4. Report it — in the US at IdentityTheft.gov, and see our reporting guide for other countries.
