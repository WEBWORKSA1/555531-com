First: if you lost money, call your bank

Before any report, contact your bank, card issuer or payment app using the number on your card or in the official app. Ask them to stop or recall the payment and flag the account. Minutes matter for wire transfers and card payments. Then change any passwords you shared and, if you gave out government ID numbers, consider freezing your credit.

What to write down

  • Date and time of the call (your call log has it).
  • The number on your caller ID — even if you suspect it was spoofed.
  • Any callback number, website, payment handle or account the caller gave you.
  • Who they claimed to be and what they asked for.
  • How much you paid, how, and to whom — if anything.

United States

  • ReportFraud.ftc.gov — the main report for scams of every kind. Reports go into the Consumer Sentinel Network used by law enforcement.
  • DoNotCall.gov — unwanted sales calls and robocalls; the FTC publishes reported numbers daily so call-blocking companies can use them.
  • IC3.gov — the FBI’s portal, especially if money moved online, through crypto, or the scam crossed borders.
  • FCC consumer complaints — caller-ID spoofing and carrier issues.
  • Impersonation of a specific agency (IRS, Social Security) can also be reported to that agency’s inspector general.

Canada

  • Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre — online reporting system or phone; also report to your local police if you lost money.
  • National Do Not Call List — complaints about telemarketers calling after the 31-day window.
  • Forward spam texts to 7726 (SPAM) — supported by Canadian carriers.

United Kingdom

  • Report Fraud (England, Wales and Northern Ireland) — the City of London Police service that replaced Action Fraud in December 2025: reportfraud.police.uk or 0300 123 2040.
  • Scotland — call Police Scotland on 101.
  • Scam calls and texts — forward texts to 7726; for calls, text the word “Call” followed by the number to 7726.
  • 159 — a short number that connects you safely to most UK banks if you think a call about your account is fake.

Australia

  • Scamwatch — report the scam and get guidance.
  • ReportCyber (cyber.gov.au) — if the scam involved cybercrime or you lost money online.
  • Contact IDCARE, Australia’s identity support service, if your personal details were exposed.

India

  • National Cyber Crime helpline 1930 — call immediately for financial fraud; fast reporting improves the chance of freezing funds.
  • cybercrime.gov.in — file a complaint online.
  • Sanchar Saathi (Chakshu) — report suspected fraud calls and messages to the Department of Telecommunications.

Does reporting actually help?

Yes, but not as a refund service. Agencies rarely respond to individual reports; they aggregate them to spot campaigns, identify payment accounts and phone carriers involved, and bring cases. The FTC estimates reported losses are only a fraction of real losses, so every report sharpens the picture. Watch the FTC’s one-minute explainer on our videos page.

Beware “recovery” scams

After you report, you may be contacted by someone promising to recover your money for a fee — sometimes claiming to be from a government agency or law firm. Agencies don’t charge to recover funds. It’s a second scam aimed at people who already lost money.
