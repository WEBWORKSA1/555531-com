Letters on a dial

Before all-digit dialling, North American phone numbers began with an exchange name — PEnnsylvania 6-5000, KLondike 5-1234. The first two letters were dialled as digits. The 5 key carried J, K and L, and very few real place names or words start with those letter pairs, so the “55” exchange space was almost empty. That made it the natural home for numbers that would never connect to anyone.

From TV studio to universal joke

By the 1960s telephone companies were encouraging film and television producers to use 555 for on-screen numbers, protecting real subscribers from fans and pranksters. Over decades the convention became so familiar that audiences recognise “555” instantly — sometimes to the point that it breaks the illusion, which is why some directors still prefer real numbers they control.

The catch: most 555 numbers aren’t fake

In the 1990s the 555 exchange was opened for genuine information services, and since then most of the range has been available for assignment. Today:

  • 555-0100 through 555-0199 are the only numbers reserved specifically for fictional use, in every area code.
  • 555-1212 remains directory assistance.
  • Any other 555 number is not guaranteed to be unassigned.
  • The 555 convention does not apply to toll-free codes — a toll-free number with 555 in the middle can be a real line.

When studios got it wrong

Using a working number can flood its owner with calls. Famous cases include films and TV shows that had to change or re-dub a number for later releases after real people were swamped. The 1981 song “867-5309/Jenny” showed the same effect from the music world: people with that number were called for years. The lesson for creators is simple — use a reserved range.

Fake numbers around the world

  • United Kingdom: Ofcom reserves 1,000-number blocks for drama in major cities (e.g. London 020 7946 0000–0999), the area-less 01632 960000–960999, and mobile numbers starting 07700 900.
  • Ireland: ComReg sets aside 020 91X XXXX.
  • Australia: ACMA publishes a list of mobile, freephone and landline numbers for creative works.
  • Germany: the Federal Network Agency reserved “drama number” blocks in several large cities.
