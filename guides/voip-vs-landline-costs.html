The market has already moved

Global VoIP services were estimated at roughly $160 billion in 2025 and are forecast to more than double by 2032, according to Persistence Market Research. Meanwhile, many carriers are retiring copper networks, which pushes up the cost and reduces the support of traditional business landlines (often called POTS lines).

A worked example

For example, an eight-person office paying $55 per line on legacy service spends $440 a month. Moving to a $25-per-user cloud plan costs $200 — a saving of $240 a month, or $2,880 a year. Over a 36-month term that is $8,640, before any hardware. Plug your own numbers into the calculator.

The hidden costs to ask about

  • Taxes and regulatory fees — VoIP bills include federal, state or provincial and local fees that can add noticeably to the advertised plan price. Ask for an all-in monthly figure.
  • Add-ons — call recording storage, AI features, toll-free minutes, contact-centre seats, fax.
  • Contract terms — annual billing discounts versus early-termination charges.
  • Internet — you may need a better connection or a backup link.

Trade-offs to take seriously

  • Power and internet outages: VoIP goes down with your internet. Mobile apps and automatic call forwarding to cell phones solve most of this.
  • Emergency calls: make sure each user’s 911/E911 address is registered, especially for remote staff.
  • Alarm, elevator and fax lines: some legacy devices need special adapters or “POTS replacement” services.
  • Call quality: test during the trial on your own network at busy times.
