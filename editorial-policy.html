Editorial policy & methodology

How we decide what to publish, how we check it, and how our tools work.

Sources first

We build guides on primary sources: regulators (FTC, FCC, CRTC, Ofcom, ACMA, ComReg), law-enforcement reports (FBI IC3, Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre), numbering administrators (NANPA) and published industry indices such as the YouMail Robocall Index. Every guide lists its sources. Statistics carry the year they describe.

Independence

Advertisers, sponsors and referral partners have no input into guides or tool logic. Sponsored placements are labelled. If we earn a referral fee from a recommendation, we say so on that page.

How the tools work

  • Number Inspector uses libphonenumber-js (Google’s open numbering metadata) for validity, type and formatting, plus our North American area-code table and documented rules for fictional, premium, toll-free, Caribbean and N11 patterns. It never contacts a server about your number.
  • Scam Call Checker weighs 12 behavioural signals drawn from FTC and CAFC scam guidance. Weights favour signals that almost never appear in legitimate calls (gift-card payment, code requests, remote access).
  • 31-Day Quiet Line adds 31 calendar days to your registration date, per US and Canadian do-not-call rules.

Corrections

Found an error? Tell us. We correct factual errors promptly and note material changes at the bottom of the page.

Review cycle

Scam and statistics guides are reviewed at least every six months and whenever a regulator publishes new annual data. Numbering data is refreshed when new area codes come into service.

AI use

We may use AI tools to help draft, translate or summarise. A human editor reviews every published page for accuracy, sourcing and tone.
