Scam or not?

1. A voicemail says your Social Security number is “suspended” and to press 1 to speak to an officer.

Scam. Government agencies don’t suspend ID numbers by robocall or ask you to press 1.

2. Your “grandson” calls crying: he’s in jail, needs bail in gift cards, and begs you not to tell his parents.

Scam. Secrecy + urgency + gift cards. Hang up and call him or his parents on a known number. Use a family safe word.

3. You call the number on the back of your card; the agent asks you to confirm your date of birth before discussing your account.

Legit. You initiated the call to a number you found yourself — identity checks are normal here.

4. “Bank security” calls about a fraud alert and asks you to read back the 6-digit code they just texted you.

Scam. Never share a one-time code with anyone who contacted you — it lets them log in as you.

5. A missed call from +1 876 rings once at 2 a.m. No voicemail.

Likely scam (one-ring). 876 is Jamaica — calling back may incur international charges.

6. “Microsoft support” says your PC is infected and asks you to install a remote-access app so they can fix it.

Scam. Tech companies don’t cold-call about infections. Remote access gives them your computer and banking.

7. Your pharmacy’s automated line calls to say your prescription is ready and gives no instructions except store hours.

Legit (informational call). It asks for nothing — no payment, no data, no urgency.

8. You’ve “won” a cruise; just pay a $199 port fee by wire transfer today to claim it.

Scam. Real prizes don’t require payment to collect.

9. Your caller ID shows your own phone number calling you.

Spoofed. Scammers fake caller ID — including your own number — to get you curious.

10. A utility “disconnection team” says power will be cut in 30 minutes unless you pay by crypto ATM.

Scam. Utilities send written notices and don’t take crypto under a 30-minute deadline.
